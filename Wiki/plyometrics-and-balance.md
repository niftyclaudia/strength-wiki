# Plyometrics, Balance & Core for Distance Runners

What the research says about hops and jumps, single-leg balance and core training during a marathon block, and how this program doses them.

**Bottom line:** Plyometrics have the best evidence of the three and are worth a few minutes twice a week. Balance work is cheap, low-risk and probably helpful for ankles, but runner-specific evidence is weak. Core training builds a stronger trunk, but its effect on running performance is mixed. A small amount is enough.

---

## Plyometrics (hops, bounds, jumps)

**What they do:** train the tendons and calves to store and return energy like a spring (the "stretch-shortening cycle"). That makes each stride a little cheaper.

**Evidence:**
- **Llanos-Lagos et al. (2024, *Sports Medicine*)**, a meta-analysis of strength-training methods in middle- and long-distance runners: plyometric training improved running economy at speeds ≤ 12 km/h (about 8:00/mi and slower) with a small effect. Heavy strength training (> 80% 1RM) was most effective across a wider range of speeds, including faster ones. Certainty of evidence: moderate.
- **Eihara et al. (2022, *Sports Medicine – Open*)**, heavy resistance vs plyometric training: heavy resistance training had *larger* effects than plyometrics on both running economy and time-trial performance, especially with near-maximal loads (≥ 90% 1RM) for 10+ weeks.

**What that means for this program:** my lifting caps at RPE 7 (about 70–80% 1RM), below the loads that showed the biggest economy gains. Plyometrics are a low-fatigue way to get some of that benefit back, especially at marathon paces. Phase 2's heavier, lower-rep work (e.g. 3–5 reps at RPE 7) moves the lifting closer to the heavy range too.

**Dosing (in this program):**
| Phase | Ground contacts / session | Drills |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (intro) | Tue ~60, Thu ~40 (low-intensity) | Pogos, single-leg pogos, squat jumps, A-skips |
| 1 (base) | Tue 60 → 80–100 | + forward single-leg hops, lateral hops, box jumps |
| 2 (build) | ~80–100 | + bounding, low drop jumps |
| 3 (peak) | ~40 | Familiar drills only |
| 4 (taper) | 0–20 | Pogos or nothing |

Rules: do plyos fresh (right after the warm-up, before lifting), stop a set when landings get loud, and no plyos the day before or after the long run.

## Single-leg balance

**Evidence:** across all sports, balance/proprioception training reduces injuries, especially ankle sprains. In runners specifically, **Wu et al. (2024, *Sports Medicine*)** found exercise-based injury-prevention programs didn't significantly reduce running injuries overall. Only *supervised* programs did, likely because people stuck with them.

**Takeaway:** consistency matters more than the specific drill. Keep it short and built into sessions: 30s eyes-closed single-leg holds and single-leg balance reaches in the plyo block. Single-leg strength work (calf raises, split squats, lunges) counts too.

## Core

**Evidence:** reviews show core training reliably improves trunk strength and endurance, but effects on performance are mixed and studies vary a lot. In runners, **Sato & Mokha (2009, *JSCR*)** found 6 weeks of core training improved 5,000 m time without changing running mechanics.

**Takeaway:** keep a small, steady dose: dead bug (Tue warm-up), side plank (Thu), Pallof press and suitcase carry (Sun). Anti-rotation and carries transfer better to running than crunches.
