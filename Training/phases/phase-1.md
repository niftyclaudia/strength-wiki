## Rules Around Running

1. **Heavy legs on Tuesday only.** Nothing loaded for legs Thu–Sat.
2. **Same-day run + lift:** run first, lift later. Space them 6+ hours apart if possible.
3. **Never past RPE 7 (3 reps left).** If you're sore enough that it changes your running stride, the weight was too heavy.
4. **If a run and a lift conflict, the run wins** (except the Tuesday lower day, which is worth protecting).
5. **Heavy legs on Thursday's run?** Drop the hip thrust 10 lb next Tuesday.

## Possible later progression — review November 2–9

Requested October 18: explore hip-flexor raises, single-leg RDLs, explosive step-ups, reverse lunge to step-up, and Bulgarian split squats. These are candidates, not newly scheduled exercises.

My practical recommendation: after another 2–3 weeks of consistent lifting, review soreness, technique and key-run quality rather than progress solely by date. Replace an existing accessory with one controlled unilateral movement first; do not add all five or increase total session volume. Keep one familiar squat/hinge and calf work. Choose either a split squat/reverse lunge or a single-leg RDL initially. Hip-flexor raises can be a small optional accessory. Learn ordinary step-ups before making them explosive; introduce power work fresh and at low volume on Tuesday, without stacking it on the existing hop/jump volume. Keep loaded legs off Thursday–Saturday under the current schedule.

Evidence supports progressive strength and power training as categories, not superiority of this exact exercise list. A 2024 review of 31 studies found benefits for running economy with high-load and some plyometric/combined methods, with moderate-to-low certainty; women were under-researched. [Llanos-Lagos et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11052887/). Direct hip-flexor research cited here tested sprint/shuttle outcomes, not marathon performance: [Deane et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16095411/). This timing and exercise sequence are coaching judgment, not a trial-proven 2–3-week rule.

## Progression

When every set of a lift hits the top of its rep range with reps to spare for its target RPE, add weight next session (Marathon HQ's Lift tab suggests +5 lb):
- Squat / RDL / hip thrust: +5–10 lb
- Press / row: +2.5–5 lb
- Kettlebell moves: add reps first, then size up the bell

Plyos: 60 → 80–100 contacts on Tuesday over the phase. Add forward single-leg hops, lateral hops, then box jumps, one at a time ([[Wiki/plyometrics-and-balance]]).
